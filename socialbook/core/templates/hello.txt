dada sahib falke....who

Dadasaheb Phalke (born Dhundiraj Govind Phalke) is universally revered as the "Father of Indian Cinema."

He was a visionary director, producer, and screenwriter who single-handedly birthed the Indian film industry at the beginning of the 20th century.

Here is a quick breakdown of why he is such a legendary figure:

1. The Pioneer of Indian Cinema
In 1913, Phalke made Raja Harishchandra, which was India's very first full-length feature film.

It was a silent film based on Hindu mythology.

Because acting wasn't considered a respectable profession for women at the time, Phalke actually had to cast a delicate-featured man (Anna Salunke) to play the leading female role.

2. A One-Man Army
Making movies back then was purely experimental. Phalke didn't just direct; he was the producer, writer, cinematographer, editor, and makeup artist. Over his 19-year career, he went on to make 95 feature-length films and 26 short films, establishing the groundwork for what would eventually become Bollywood and the broader Indian film ecosystem.

3. The Highest Honor in Indian Cinema
To honor his massive legacy, the Government of India introduced the Dadasaheb Phalke Award in 1969.

It is the highest, most prestigious award in Indian cinema, presented annually at the National Film Awards for "outstanding contribution to the growth and development of Indian cinema."
